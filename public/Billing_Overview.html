Billing Analytics

Invoicing, collections and realization across Haven publications — what we billed, what came in, and what leaked.

Xero invoices by stage

Every invoice ever written to Xero_Invoice__c, by its current stage. Total = deals total plus credits applied (credits are negative), so it is the amount actually owed on the invoice.

Invoices written per month

Invoices by the month they were created in Salesforce, split by where they ended up. The bulk of October 2025 is the initial Xero backfill, not a month of real invoicing — read the months after that as the live run-rate.

Voided invoices over time

Void rate = voided ÷ (voided + awaiting payment + paid + authorised) for invoices created that month. A void undoes an invoice entirely, so a rising line means billing is being re-cut rather than collected.

Outstanding invoice aging

Open invoices (Awaiting Payment, Authorised, Payment Failed, Needs Review) bucketed by how far past their Due Date they are as of today. "Not yet due" is money still inside terms.

Outstanding by market

Open invoice value by the market stamped on the invoice. Market is only filled in on the newer Xero records — the backfilled batch carries none, so it lands in "Unassigned". Treat this as a view of recent invoicing, not the full book.

Invoices paid, by month written

Xero invoices now sitting in Paid, grouped by the month the invoice was created. Salesforce does not store a payment date on the Xero record, so this shows which cohorts of invoicing have converted — not the day cash landed.

Deal-level dollars collected, slate by slate

Rolling up Paid Amount on approved deals by the slate their issue launched in — the closest thing to a cash-in curve the org keeps. The green bars start partway along on purpose: Paid Amount was not populated before Haven moved billing onto the Xero/Stripe sync, so earlier slates are blank rather than zero.

Booked vs invoiced vs collected, per slate

For every approved deal on a slate: Booked is deal Amount, Invoiced is Invoiced Amount, Collected is Paid Amount. The gap between booked and collected is the leakage — discounts, stop-invoice deals, write-offs and money still in flight. Two things to read past: the green series only begins where Paid Amount started being written, and the last few slates have not finished collecting.

Realization rate over time

Invoiced rate = invoiced ÷ booked. Collection rate = collected ÷ booked. Both are per-slate. The invoiced line runs the full window; the collection line starts at the billing-system cutover. Slates inside the last ~120 days are still maturing, so the falling right edge is expected, not a warning.

Realization by publication

Last 12 months of approved deals, grouped by the issue's market label. Sorted by collection rate — the bottom of this table is where booked revenue is least likely to turn into cash.

Revenue per product, per month

Line-item revenue on approved deals, grouped by the month their issue launched. Products priced at $0 (identifiers like Camera-Ready, Re-Run, Premium Placement) carry volume but no revenue — switch the measure to see that split. The final bar or two are partial: those issues have not finished selling.

Product revenue, period over period

Twelve complete launch months against the twelve before, by product. Change is the dollar swing — use it to see which units are carrying the book and which have quietly gone to zero. The current month is excluded on both sides, since its issues are still being sold.

Extra copies billed, slate by slate

All line items whose product name contains Extra Copies, on approved deals. Copies and dollars are plotted together because they have decoupled: volume is up sharply while revenue per copy stays near zero.

By product tier and by market

The numbered tiers (5/10/15/30 Extra Copies) are the retired products; the generic Extra Copies and Extra Copies (Cover Wrap Overrun) lines are what is used now. Market table covers the last 12 months.

Deals marked "Stop Invoice", per slate

The Prepaid__c field (labelled Stop Invoice) set to Yes on an approved deal — billing is deliberately suppressed, usually because the deal is covered by a subscription or was paid up front. Shown as a count and as a share of all approved deals on that slate. The last point or two drop sharply because those slates are still being sold and flagged, not because behaviour changed.

Stop Invoice by publication

Last 12 months. A market well above the house average is either subscription-heavy or has a rep suppressing invoices that should be going out — worth a spot check either way.

Refunds over time

Refunded Stripe invoices by the date Stripe created the original invoice. Switch between weekly and monthly buckets.

Refunds by originating slate

Each refunded invoice traced back through its agreement to that agreement's first deal, and then to that deal's issue launch date. It attributes the refund to where the relationship started, not necessarily the issue being refunded — useful for spotting bad cohorts, not for per-issue accounting.

Stripe subscriptions by status

Live subscription records. Monthly price sums the recurring price on each subscription, so the Active row is the closest thing to current MRR in the org.

Collections queue

Agreements by Collections Status. Value is the agreement's total value; collected is total paid against it. Agreements with no collections status set are excluded from the chart but shown in the table.
