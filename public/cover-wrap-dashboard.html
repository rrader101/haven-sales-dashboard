Getting a CSV URL from Drive. Open the sheet → File → Share → Publish to web → pick the tab → Comma-separated values (.csv) → Publish. Paste the link above. A single Sheet with two tabs works well: one tab for deals, one for sends.

Keeping it current. Point a Zap at the Drive folder — trigger New File in Folder, action Google Sheets → Create Spreadsheet Row(s) or Replace File against that same Sheet. The published URL never changes, so this page always reads the latest drop.

Cover Wrap Overrun — No-Listing Email Campaign

Agents with no active listing get a weekly email pitching a cover wrap of themselves or their brokerage. This tracks which recipients went on to run a Cover Wrap Overrun deal, and what each email was worth. Sends come from the YAMM slate sheets in Drive; deals from Salesforce, joined on contact email.

Revenue booked over time

Revenue per email sent

Booked revenue in a month ÷ emails sent that month. This is the yield number — what one email into the no-listing list is worth. Shown on the wave and send-month axes, since emails are sent on a weekly cadence, not per issue.

Where deals land

Every attributed deal by stage. Approved = ran in the issue.

How long conversion takes

Days from the email send to the opportunity being created.

Revenue by slate

Total booked revenue attributed to each slate, and what each slate’s emails are worth. A slate with modest total revenue can still be the best list if its $ per email is high — compare the two charts.

Revenue by issue

Which printed issues absorbed the cover wrap overruns these emails generated. Filter to one market, or leave on all. $ per email is not shown here — emails go out weekly to a list, not per issue, so there is no honest denominator at the issue level.

Send-month cohorts

Each month’s email volume and the revenue it went on to produce, split by slate. Recent months are still maturing — a deal can be created up to the attribution window after the send, so the right-hand bars will keep growing.

Volume vs. yield

Emails sent per month, and the revenue each one returned. Two separate scales, so they are two separate charts — read them together, not against a shared axis.

Attributed deals

Every deal matched back to an email send under the current filters. Search by name, company, slate, issue or market. Sortable on any column.

Deals file — Cover Wrap Overrun opportunities

A Salesforce report at the opportunity product level works best: one row per line item. The dashboard sums Cover Wrap Overrun lines into CWO revenue and every line into full-deal revenue. An opportunity-level export works too — just give it a CWO amount column. Column names are matched loosely, so Close Date, close_date and CloseDate all land.

Sends file — weekly no-listing emails

One row per recipient, or one row per send batch with a count. Per-recipient rows let the dashboard join deals to sends on email address; batch rows still give the denominator for $ per email, but then the deals file has to carry its own slate and send date.

NeededColumnAccepts
How attribution works. A deal is credited to the most recent email sent to that address before the opportunity was created, as long as the gap is inside the attribution window. Change the window in the controls bar to see how sensitive the numbers are to that assumption.
